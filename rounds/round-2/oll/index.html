The cartographer’s guide

How to read a lying atlas

The task

Ink a terrain into every hex. Each of the five clue types is either wholly true or wholly false, and exactly the stated number of clues are lies. When your map satisfies every honest clue and exactly the lying ones, the atlas is proven. Every generated atlas has exactly one such solution.

Tiers

  • Surveyor — radius 3, three terrains, no lies.
  • Cartographer — radius 4, four terrains, one lie.
  • Archmage — radius 6, five terrains, two lies.

Controls

  • Select a hex, then ink a terrain or toggle pencil notes.
  • Press a wax seal on a clue you believe is a lie. You have exactly as many seals as lies.
  • H asks Why? — the propagation solver names the next deduction and its supporting clue.
  • T opens the self-test (never while typing in the seed field).
  • 1–5 ink, 0 erase, Shift+1–5 pencil, Ctrl/Cmd+Z undo.
  • Arrow keys walk the hex selector. Top view flattens the camera; High/Low detail caps the pixel ratio at 2.

The five clue forms

  • Neighbour count — exactly k neighbouring hexes hold terrain T.
  • Row count — the row of equal axial r holds exactly k hexes of T.
  • Connectivity — all T hexes form one non-empty connected region.
  • Isolation — no two T hexes share an edge.
  • Twin — two named hexes hold the same terrain.

Rendering

The atlas is drawn with three.js: procedurally textured parchment, beveled ink prisms, a rippling sea shader, faceted snow-capped peaks, low-poly pines, dune ridges, swamp bubbles, a brass compass rose, and wax seals cast with LatheGeometry. Quality, top view, motion, and reduced-motion preferences are remembered locally.

All textures, models and effects are generated procedurally. Fonts are optional; the game falls back to Georgia if they cannot load.
